About DumpCheck

A dump-station list you can trust when your black tank is full.

Why this exists

Most dump-station lists are crowd-sourced and rarely cleaned up, so they’re full of stations that closed years ago. DumpCheck lists a station only when an official source confirms it: the National Park Service, the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, a state agency or the business itself. Every station shows the date we checked it and a plain status: open, call first, closed for the season or closed.

It starts with Tennessee and grows one verified station at a time.

Made in Nashville, Tennessee.

How it’s researched

Stations and guides are researched by Yeha, an AI research assistant, which re-checks sources on a schedule. Each station links to its sources. Things change, so call ahead when it matters.

Independence

DumpCheck isn’t run by or affiliated with any park, agency or business. No one pays to be listed. Some guides may include labeled affiliate links; see disclosures.
